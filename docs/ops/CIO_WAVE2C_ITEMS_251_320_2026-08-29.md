## The finding: two execution-language gates, disjoint coverage

Item 186/302 asks that research cannot create an action. Two gates enforce it —
and they have **different vocabularies**, so each was covering the other's blind
spot by accident:

| phrase | worker `EXEC_LINT` | `critique` forbidden_authority | net |
|---|---|---|---|
| `place an order` | miss (pattern is `place order`) | **FAILED** | caught |
| `buy now` | **caught** | VALID | caught |
| `execute the buy` | **miss** | **VALID** | **passed both** |

`EXEC_LINT` required exact adjacency: `place order` matched, `place an order`
did not; `execute trade` matched, `execute the buy` did not. **An article was
enough to pass the gate** — precisely the failure shape fixed next door in #631,
where `ignore all previous instructions` slipped past a one-qualifier pattern.

Two rigid regexes, written at different times, each blind to what the other
happened to catch. Nothing in either file said the other existed.

### Fixed — the article gap only

`place/submit/execute` now tolerate an optional article. Benign analysis stays
clean, which is the half that matters: *"management will execute its buyback
plan"*, *"the order book was thin"* and *"revenue execution improved this
quarter"* are all still VALID.

### Deliberately not fixed — an operator policy call

`trim the position` and `sell half the position` pass **both** gates. They are
not added, because they appear in legitimate analysis and rejecting them would
**silently shrink research coverage** (466 currently attachable). Whether
advisory verbs should be treated as execution language is your decision, not a
unilateral tightening. A test asserts the current behaviour so the choice stays
visible rather than drifting.
