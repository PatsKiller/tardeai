future increment that would size, order, stop or weight is out of scope by
construction, not by configuration.

## Maturity levels

### L1 — Provenance and liveness (reached tonight)

Every durable row carries the exact served SHA. Every scheduled producer
actually produces. A wake reaches a decision every slot.

### L2 — Grounded cognition

A wake loads memory **about its subject**, consumes research it did not
already cite, and the combination changes a named field on a named record.
The proof is a diff of `next_research_question` with and without the input,
not a count of rows.

### L3 — Judgment with a falsifier

A model is asked only on a material question, its output is validated by a
deterministic gate, and the resulting commitment states what would prove it
wrong. `stance` varies across subjects, because a stance that never varies is
a constant.

### L4 — The loop closes

Checkpoints settle, outcomes score, and a lesson derived from being wrong
changes a later question. **Until this level the desk accumulates; it does not
learn.**

### L5 — Unattended and self-reporting

Every level above, observed on its own schedule with nobody watching, and the
desk reporting its own degradations before an auditor finds them.

## What "proven" means at every level

Descending strength, from AGENTS.md §8 — **only the first two settle a claim**:

1. Observed from the served release, **unattended, on its own schedule**.
2. Observed from the served release, run by hand.
3. A mutation-tested dry run.
4. A test that can be shown to fail.
5. Reading the source.
6. A document saying so.

A staged proof message that asks the operator to reply "OK" is **level 2 at
best**, and tonight's measurement found that every gateway acceptance in the
system's history is exactly that.

## The one-sentence version

At full maturity the desk raises its own question, answers it from what it
already knows before it spends anything, commits to a claim that could be
proven wrong, tells the operator once and only when something changed, finds
out whether it was right, and gets better — **without ever touching a
position.**

## The count that matters

Not a percentage. Five proofs (§15), each observed unattended from the served
release:

```
M1 Research     the system raised it, it completed, it changed a named field
M2 Advice       a critique verdict changed the next question
M3 Feedback     an operator reply changed the next wake's behaviour
M4 Consistency  one producer per number, no two surfaces disagreeing
M5 Persistence  a disposition made days ago is still honoured, unreplayed
```

**A truthful three of five beats a claimed five.** If all five come back
observed on the first attempt, assume something is wrong and find it.
